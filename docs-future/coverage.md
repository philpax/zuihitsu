# Coverage

What this design addresses, graded honestly, and what it makes worse.

The grading is uneven on purpose. Six of the eleven surveyed failures are closed structurally, meaning the failure becomes unrepresentable rather than discouraged. Five are answered in design and rest on evidence that has not been gathered. Presenting all eleven as addressed would be the more comfortable claim and the less useful one.

The failures are those recorded in [`../docs/ontology-failures/2026-07-23.md`](../docs/ontology-failures/2026-07-23.md). The research snapshots retain their dated terminology. The ownership below is the current successor programme and does not change current-system issue state.

## Ownership vocabulary

Coverage rows use canonical ownership targets. `stage:<n>` names one numbered Stage 0–12. `capability:<name>` names a capability in the versioned genesis-selection record. `gate:<name>` names its independent activation gate. `out-of-successor/<scope>` names an explicit current-system, tooling, or other ownership boundary outside the successor architecture. `owner:<name>` names the responsible authority and must resolve through the owner register. Each row has one `owner_id`; `secondary_owner_ids` records prerequisite work without changing primary ownership. `capability_id` and `activation_gate_id` are `none` when the row has no capability or gate.

The machine-integrity rule is: each failure, issue, and obligation has a stable `record_id`, exactly one primary `owner_id`, a declared `owner_kind`, a responsible `owner_authority_id`, and explicit capability and activation-gate fields. A numbered stage or an activation gate is a successor owner. An `out-of-successor/...` target is not silently treated as successor coverage.

## Regressions

The second trace is not free, and three of the five partial answers get harder because of it.

Narrative generation is a new prompt-borne load-bearing surface. The design's rule is that generative work stays on the prompt because it tolerates drift. A narrative is generative by construction, and it is now load-bearing. The prior art's own pilot reports that a directive to use its protocol was reliably overridden by the model's trained default and had to be moved into the system prompt to take effect, which is the prompt-sensitive load-bearing behaviour reappearing inside the fix. The containment is to make the existence and linkage of a trace structural while leaving only its quality soft, and to measure content correctness rather than presence.

Narrative is a new geometry-sensitive index. The survey measured its widest similarity variance in exactly the long-text regime a narrative occupies: 0.80 against 0.94 for the same content under different prefixes, varying by length. The new index needs the same distribution-relative calibration as every other, and inherits none of the protection that structural equality gives the deduplication path.

Narrative licenses invention. The mechanism works by asking a model to commit to concrete detail it was not told. The instance has already produced the unelaborated version of that failure. This is why the [episodic wall](verified-write.md) is a critic rather than a sentence, and why it should ship before any narrative generation rather than alongside it.

## Mitigations the design erases

The survey notes that a mitigation in the current ontology is itself evidence of a workaround tax the redesign should erase. Three mitigations become structure:

- The write-time cross-subject advisory, which steers the agent around the one-subject representation, becomes the Event node. There is nothing left to steer around.
- The temporal extraction's third-party-routine rule, which teaches the model not to stamp a recurrence on a fact describing someone else's job, becomes the absence of a trigger. The rule is unnecessary because the outcome is unrepresentable.
- The current-day guard's text check, which asks whether the source utterance could have supplied today's date, becomes [span justification](verified-write.md). The current system calls that check a heuristic over text rather than a property of it, and accepts false suppressions in both directions to keep it. A span matched against the source utterance is a property of the text, so the heuristic has nothing left to approximate.

The first two are load-bearing behaviours moving from wording into structure, which is the prompt-sensitivity remedy applied to the Event and temporal contracts. The third is a heuristic being replaced by a decidable check rather than by a representation, which is a weaker kind of erasure and worth naming as one.

### Made worse

#66 (the console replica must bound its event-log mirror and time-travel window) moves from deferred to blocking. The console holds the whole log in browser memory and re-folds it from zero on every time-travel scrub, and this design multiplies the dominant term in log size: recorded model calls are already 96% of payload bytes in the live instance, and structuring adds a call per write block on top. Everything the design moves into the fold, severance filtering, alias resolution, credence derivation, frame defaulting, is then paid per scrub. A preregistered budget and measurement criterion for bytes added per turn, browser fold time at realistic log sizes, and replay cost is required before the trial. The criterion is an acceptance gate for the affected slice, not a report after implementation. A negative result returns the candidate to simplification or revision, or withholds authority. It is owned by `stage:1` as baseline evidence and `stage:2` as the reference-model consumer, with `owner:model-eval` responsible for the measurement and the genesis freeze as the blocking gate.

### Inherited, not solved

#123 (open: the present set conflates audience with participation, leaking confidences to silent channel members) is a Stage 1 witness-assurance and Stage 5 audience-resolution genesis blocker, not only a warning. The connector protocol must distinguish availability from witnessed presence, preserve `availability ⊇ presence`, record roster evidence and witness scope, and default disclosure to teller-only when it cannot. [Transmission principles](privacy-and-provenance.md) consume that record. Stage 1 audits every consumer: visibility, briefs, search, links, turn references, and scheduling. #124 and #127 cannot pass in the current system while #123 remains unresolved. A richer condition evaluated against the wrong set is a more expressive leak. The successor ownership is `stage:1` with secondary `stage:5`, `owner:privacy`, and no activation gate.

### Issues whose right fix changes

Three issues stay open and stop being what they were. Fixing them against the current model is still correct. Fixing them the way they are written would build something this design then has to undo.

#97 (a block's buffered `memory.create` colliding with a platform mint) closes in its reported form and not in general. The reported case is the agent minting a platform-qualified stub while a connector mints the same name, and under [the write surface](write-surface.md) the agent performs no handle selection and mints no qualified stub at all, so that collision becomes unrepresentable. This design says nothing about two writers racing on a name the agent can mint, which is the general race and is untouched. Its successor owner is `stage:2`, `owner:identity`, with the general race explicitly out of successor scope.

#109 (Discord reply context) proposes injecting a reference token into relayed message text. Under this design an Occasion carries a first-class [utterance](two-traces.md). Stage 2 records outbound Occasions in the same source model. A reply is a relation between Occasions rather than a token spliced into prose. A query can traverse that relation. Its successor owner is `stage:2`, `owner:source`, with no activation gate.

#110 (closed: Discord attachments) establishes the current inbound baseline. The Discord relay downloads and uploads files and announces connector download, upload, and cap failures ([relay](../platform-connectors/discord/src/bot/attachments/relay.rs#L91-L147), [relay tests](../platform-connectors/discord/src/bot/attachments/tests.rs#L225-L258), [server refusal fallback](../platform-connectors/discord/src/bot/process.rs#L85-L104), [connector documentation](../platform-connectors/discord/README.md#behaviour)). The server records filename, media type, hash, length, and `Image`/`Text`/`Opaque` classification on `ConversationTurn` ([storage contract](../docs/events-and-storage.md), [`AttachmentKind`](../crates/core/src/attachment.rs), [`ConversationTurn`](../crates/core/src/event/payload.rs)). Supported images become model image parts, text attachments are boundedly inlined, and opaque, missing, or unreadable stored blobs produce a separate server/model-render announcement class ([attachment rendering](../src/agent/turn/attachments/mod.rs#L24-L147), [attachment tests](../tests/agent/attachments.rs)). Current `ModelCalled` requests retain image hash and MIME while excluding bytes, but do not record a Perception, selector, reference authorisation, or transform lineage ([image part](../crates/core/src/model.rs#L50-L81), [request record](../crates/core/src/event/mod.rs#L527-L543), [recorder](../src/agent/turn/recording.rs#L130-L147)). The console displays recorded attachments through the blob route ([component](../console/src/views/conversation/Attachments.tsx#L10-L27), [rendering](../console/src/views/conversation/Attachments.tsx#L109-L116), [test](../console/src/views/conversation/Attachments.test.tsx#L62-L75)), and replay can present the same image while its blob remains available. These current capabilities close #110's connector upload, durable recording, conversational model perception, console display, relay failures, render failures, and replay baseline. They do not provide ontology integration, durable [Perceptions](artefacts-and-perceptions.md), governed historical reinspection, visual-content retrieval, or general document and media ingestion. The blob store is not reconstructed from the event log and is backed up with it, and the blob route treats a content hash as a bearer capability; both are accepted for a server that is not publicly exposed ([storage contract](../docs/events-and-storage.md#blob-store)). The current semantic search and Lua surfaces expose no such capability ([search implementation](../src/memory/search.rs), [memory API reference](../src/agent/lua/reference/memory.rs), [Lua tables](../src/agent/lua/tables/), [control API construction](../src/instance/control/actions.rs), and [control API test](../src/http_server/tests/api/control.rs)). The successor records the Artefact, ArtefactReference, Perception, locator, audience, and derivation seams at genesis. Stage 1 measures prior Perception retrieval against explicit reinspection and textual memory. Stage 6 owns the artefact, erasure, and recovery substrate. `capability:bulk-ingestion` is an independent activation gate, and `capability:historical-reinspection`, `capability:ocr`, `capability:generated-captions`, `capability:region-grounding`, `capability:visual-retrieval`, and `capability:scene-graph-writer` each have independent activation gates. Blob bytes remain outside log reconstruction and require backup with the log ([current blob contract](../docs/events-and-storage.md)). #132 is therefore independent of closed #110: #110 closes the current inbound attachment baseline, while #132 proposes a successor-facing read surface whose authorisation, selectors, lineage, and API/reference/scaffold wiring still require its own gate. The #110 closure does not satisfy, select, or block that gate.
