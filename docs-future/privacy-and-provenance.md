# Privacy and provenance

Privacy constrains information flow. It is not a tag on semantic content. [Attestations and direct SourceAuthorities](statements.md) carry transmission principles and source locators or typed observation edges. [Assertions](statements.md) receive audience-safe testimonial support from visible Attestations, while a direct Assertion remains usable only through its visible live SourceAuthority. [Perceptions and artefact references](artefacts-and-perceptions.md) carry the same restrictions through multimodal work. A [Derivation](statements.md) records the restrictions and resolution environment under which its output was produced.

The design rule is to record witness and influence evidence broadly and immutably, then interpret it under narrow, versioned policies. Contextual integrity supports transmission principles as conditions over an information-flow trace, but the exact vocabulary and compilation rules below are design decisions ([research lane](research/2026-07-24/lanes/provenance-privacy.md#4-contextual-integrity-transmission-principles-as-first-class-data)).

## Transmission principles

Each Attestation carries one registered, versioned principle. Genesis reserves the representation for later principles, but the initial evaluator accepts only semantics it can decide cheaply and fail closed.

| Initial principle | Compiled condition |
|---|---|
| `public` | Any authenticated audience is eligible, subject to other authorisation and erasure rules. |
| `attributed` | Eligible only when the rendered result preserves the teller attribution. |
| `demonstrated_witness_only` | Every audience member must have qualifying witness evidence for the source Occasion. `in_confidence` is an agent-facing name for this principle. |
| `include(S)` | Every audience member must resolve to a member of `S`. |
| `exclude(S)` | No audience member may resolve to a member of `S`. |
| `consent(scope, expiry, revocation)` | A signed consent record must include every audience member and the proposed flow; it must be unexpired and unretracted. |

Principles compose by conjunction. Evaluation is universally quantified over the current audience, and an unknown identity or unresolvable condition denies. A group is eligible only when every member is eligible. Partial rendering is permitted only where the object type defines a disclosure-safe projection; otherwise the whole object is suppressed.

Reciprocity and purpose limitation remain gated extensions. Reciprocity needs a stable definition of comparable disclosure. Purpose limitation needs an execution-purpose and obligation model that constrains downstream use, not a string supplied by the caller. General temporal logic is not on the read path.

## Witness evidence

An Occasion records witness evidence rather than an unqualified participant set. Each item names a person or platform stub, an assurance kind, its connector or operator source, recorded time, and a mandatory witness scope. The scope is one utterance span, an ordered typed-content-part range, an ArtefactReference, a delivery or acknowledgement target, or an explicitly certified whole Occasion. Whole-Occasion certification requires a connector or operator assurance kind registered for that purpose; it is never inferred from participation.

| Assurance kind | Meaning | May widen disclosure? | May suppress independence? |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `teller` | The person supplied the Attestation. | yes | yes |
| `active_participant` | The connector demonstrates contribution to the relevant span. | yes | yes |
| `explicit_acknowledgement` | The person acknowledged the content or relevant span. | yes | yes |
| `delivered` | The connector reports delivery. | no | yes |
| `channel_member` | The person was a member of the channel. | no | yes |
| `unknown` | Presence cannot be established. | no | no |

A versioned disclosure policy derives the narrow disclosure set. It starts with the teller and widens only for `active_participant` or `explicit_acknowledgement`; a future connector-specific policy may admit another assurance only after its semantics are demonstrated. Widening applies only when the witness scope covers every source locator supporting the Attestation. A compound Occasion with evidence for one span or content part does not license another. Partial coverage falls back to teller-only. A versioned exposure policy derives a conservative upper bound from teller, participation, acknowledgement, delivery, and channel membership. Exposure is read only by dependence analysis and can only suppress apparent independence. Channel membership never licenses disclosure.

This preserves the asymmetric rule established by the earlier design: evidence that only suppresses may be conservative, while evidence that licenses a flow must be demonstrated. Dynamic presence remains an admitted evidence gap, so teller-only is the fallback ([research lane](research/2026-07-24/lanes/provenance-privacy.md#implications-for-zuihitsu); [confidence register](confidence.md#privacy-and-provenance)).

## Subject guard

The subject guard compiles into an additional negative recipient predicate for every Attestation. It never widens an audience.

The compiler is the pure function `source_guard(query_scope, audience_member, source_evidence_id, policy_version, resolution_environment, source_head)`, where `source_evidence_id` resolves to exactly one Attestation, SourceAuthority, or Derivation whose typed output is the Assertion being considered. `query_scope` is one of: an exact Assertion ID; an exact Event ID; an exact composite-resolution ID; or a bounded candidate set already selected by non-semantic keys such as namespace, source sequence interval, and registered relation or Event-type IDs. It may not be narrowed by rendered values, support, rank, visibility, or the guard itself. The function reads the immutable log prefix through `source_head` and returns only `allow` or `deny` plus an operator-only decision trace. A missing, wrong-type, erased, invalidated, or unresolved source-evidence record returns `deny`.

1. Form the candidate record set. For an Assertion scope, include that Assertion. For an Event scope, include every non-erased role and attribute Assertion whose subject or object is that Event ID. For a composite scope, take the union for every member Event in the recorded composite. For a bounded candidate scope, take the union for every selected Assertion, Event, and live composite that the query could return before audience filtering. Include `candidate`, `settled`, `superseded`, `retracted`, and `invalidated` records when their retained coordinates can identify a protected person; exclude only terminally erased coordinate payload, which contributes an opaque deny marker whenever its tombstone says that a protected person position was erased. This set is independent of the final projection.
2. Extract protected subject positions under `policy_version`. They are every Proposition subject whose registered entity-kind constraint admits a person; every person-valued Event role designated `participant`, `actor`, `patient`, `experiencer`, or a registered subrole whose parent is one of those roles; and every person-valued attribute definition marked `subject_guarded`. A definition with unknown kind, missing historical version, or erased coordinates contributes an opaque deny marker. Non-person locations, organisations, quantities, and literals do not.
3. Expand each extracted handle through the identity component of `resolution_environment`. An accepted disclosure-cleared composite contributes all member stubs. Every overlapping, conflicting, or candidate hypothesis touching an extracted handle contributes all possible member stubs to the deny set but never to an allow decision. Unknown membership contributes an opaque deny marker. Expansion is monotone and never removes a subject.
4. Evaluate the named source evidence's transmission principle or, for a Derivation, its output principle and InfluenceEnvelope intersection. For an Attestation, remove its teller from the deny set only for delivery of that teller's own Attestation and only when the principle itself permits that delivery. Remove another protected person only when disclosure-qualifying `active_participant` or `explicit_acknowledgement` evidence identifies that same resolved person and covers every source locator supporting this Attestation. For a SourceAuthority or Derivation, there is no teller exception: an authenticated agent, operator, tool, or producing Activity is provenance and authority, not evidence that a protected subject witnessed or consented to the flow. A non-testimonial source may remove a protected person only through the same explicit scoped witness evidence, and one with no Occasion witness edge removes nobody. The fact that a subject participated in an Occasion is not enough unless the scoped witness evidence demonstrates coverage. Channel membership, delivery alone, and possible identity do not qualify.
5. Return `deny` when `audience_member` resolves to any remaining protected stub, when identity resolution is ambiguous with one, when the source evidence is not live and readable, or when an opaque deny marker applies to the audience's possible identity. Otherwise return the source evidence's transmission-principle result. Only after every Attestation and SourceAuthority is evaluated may the resolver aggregate support and construct a disclosure-safe Event projection. Omission, incomplete-shell, and suppression operate only in this final phase.

| Guard vector | Candidate closure | Audience and exception | Expected result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subject is the teller | The Assertion subject resolves to `person/P`; its only Attestation teller is `person/P`. | The principle permits teller delivery. | `allow` for this Attestation only. Another teller's Attestation receives no exception. |
| Subject participated | An Event has `person/P` in a protected participant subrole and another person tells the claim. | `person/P` appears in the Occasion, but no scoped active-participant or acknowledgement evidence covers every locator. | `deny`; participation metadata alone does not license disclosure. |
| Multi-subject Event | Protected roles resolve to `person/P` and `person/Q`; a public role would remain after projection. | The caller is `person/Q`; no qualifying witness exception exists. | `deny` before projection. Omitting Q's hidden role cannot turn the Event into an allowed result. |
| Tentative overlapping identity | A protected handle participates in accepted composite `[P1,P2]` and overlapping candidate `[P2,P3]`. | The caller resolves possibly to `P3`. | `deny`; candidate expansion widens only the deny set. |
| Hidden role omitted from final projection | A candidate Event contains a retained confidential participant role and an independently public attribute. | The caller is the confidential participant. | `deny` from the pre-render closure even if the final projection would omit the role. |
| Erased protected coordinate | A retained tombstone says that a person-valued protected role was erased, but its handle is unavailable. | The caller's non-membership cannot be proved. | `deny` through the opaque marker; an authorised operator may inspect only the decision trace and permitted tombstone. |
| Direct agent observation | A SourceAuthority supports an Assertion whose subject is `person/P`; it has no Occasion witness edge. | The caller is `person/P`; actor is the agent and principle is public. | `deny`; the agent actor receives no teller exception and public does not bypass the subject guard. |
| Direct operator assertion | A SourceAuthority supports an Assertion whose subject is not the operator and carries `include([operator])/v1`. | The caller is the authenticated operator. | `allow` only when the operator is not in the protected closure; another protected caller is denied. |
| Derived tool observation | A Derivation outputs an Event role Assertion naming `person/P`; its tool observation and Activity have no person witness evidence. | The caller resolves possibly to `person/P`. | `deny`; tool execution, Derivation authority, and a public output principle do not establish witness or consent. |

The guard is evaluated per Attestation, SourceAuthority, or Assertion-producing Derivation before support aggregation or direct-source use. One teller's clearance cannot expose another teller's restricted support for the same Assertion, and direct actor authority cannot create a subject exception. The candidate-set and expansion rules are monotone, non-rendering, and fail-closed. They remove the circular dependency between subject discovery and audience-safe Event rendering. The policy version, scope, source head, source-evidence ID, and ResolutionEnvironment are recorded because later reinterpretation could disclose existing history.

## Audience-safe support and zero residue

An uncleared input must leave no observable residue. Text, dates, counts, rankings, ordinals, omissions, queue activity, tool calls, and initiated actions are all observations. The current system demonstrated the problem when a visible link row carried a date from a withheld entry even though no hidden text was rendered ([current-system evidence](research/2026-08-06/current-system-fixes.md#redaction-decided-per-read-path)). Central audience resolution is therefore required before rendering.

The store may compute a global epistemic support projection for operator audit, but it is not an audience-safe input to conversational decisions. For an audience, actionable support is recomputed from visible Attestations after subject-guard and transmission evaluation. Any ordinal, ordering, recommendation, Derivation, or initiated action must either:

- depend only on audience-safe inputs; or
- inherit the intersection of every influencing input's transmission restrictions and remain hidden unless that intersection clears.

This rule removes the hidden-endorsement leak. Hidden evidence cannot change a visible rank or ordinal while its existence remains concealed. Global support may schedule restricted internal review, but the review item and every result retain the hidden influence envelope.

Descriptions are derived only from inputs whose principle is `public` and that also pass subject guards, erasure, retention, and every other authorisation check, because their ordinary surface has no attribution or audience gate. `public` is never a bypass. Generated episodes and other audience-gated syntheses inherit the intersection of their inputs and retain structural attribution. Neither is evidence independent of its sources.

Zero residue is this design's application of non-interference. The research supports the information-flow framing; the complete compilation and central resolver are safety synthesis that require scenario testing ([research lane](research/2026-07-24/lanes/provenance-privacy.md#4-contextual-integrity-transmission-principles-as-first-class-data)).

## Influence envelopes

Influence is broader than accepted Assertions. Every Activity and derived result records an InfluenceEnvelope containing the typed input-edge IDs, input transmission-principle versions, intersected restriction expression, audience-decision ID, purpose definition/version, ResolutionEnvironment, source head, and any erasure or retention dependencies. This applies to:

- model prompts and model-call records;
- accepted, rejected, amended, and dropped proposals;
- critic diagnostics and retry context;
- queue items, leases, poison records, and working notes;
- Perceptions, OCR, captions, embeddings, and visual descriptions;
- Derivations, support calculations, and negative query results;
- descriptions, generated episodes, and other summaries;
- transformed or generated Artefacts;
- eval fixtures and packages built from real records.

Rejection does not erase influence: a rejected proposal may change a retry or teach a later decision what not to say. Implementations may compact payloads, but they must preserve enough typed lineage and restriction data to reproduce authorisation and erasure closure. Access is accounted when content is rendered into model or tool context, including retries, not when it merely appears in a hidden candidate set.

## Provenance and derivation

A versioned Activity executes work and can produce zero or more Derivations. Each Derivation is a separate immutable result and lineage record, not a note attached after a conclusion. Its typed inputs may include Assertions, Perceptions, tool or direct observations, aggregates, negative query results, and policy or schema definitions. It records implementation and criterion versions, ontology versions, audience and support policies, identity-resolution environment, assumptions, source head sequence, one typed output, and influence envelope. A retry is a new Activity and cannot overwrite an earlier Derivation. See the canonical [Derivation definition](statements.md#derivation).

This extends the PROV-shaped entity/activity/agent lineage with defeasible dependencies. PROV supports recorded lineage, but resolution environments and complete influence taint are this design's synthesis ([research lane](research/2026-07-24/lanes/provenance-privacy.md#the-derivationprovenance-record-failure-class-11-94-100)). “How do you know?” returns complete recorded lineage where available and an audit trace otherwise; it is not described as a proof.

Identity and Event resolution are not represented by a single merge stamp. A Derivation names the complete resolution environment it read. Withdrawal of any accepted hypothesis invalidates outputs produced under that environment. New evidence can mark an output as owing recomputation; weakened or withdrawn support can mark it for re-evaluation. The fold remains deterministic, while model-driven re-derivation is a later recorded Activity.

## Retraction and erasure

Retraction and erasure are separate appended operations.

- Retraction or supersession changes folded live state while retaining content for audit. Retracting an Attestation removes that teller's support, not the Assertion or other Attestations.
- Erasure destroys or cryptographically shreds governed payloads. The hash-chained envelope and authorized erasure record may remain, and replay is deterministic over what survives.

Crypto-shredding and forgettable payloads are established event-sourcing patterns. Their exact combination with a tamper-evident envelope and derivation invalidation is a design synthesis ([research lane](research/2026-07-24/lanes/provenance-privacy.md#5-forgetting-vs-append-only-reconciling-erasure-with-deterministic-replay)).

Every erasure begins with an immutable authorization decision consumed by closure traversal.

| Requester | Authentication | Permitted scope and operation | Conflict and appeal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| teller | Connector or operator binding to the Attestation identity | Retract their Attestation; request erasure of payload they supplied where retention law permits | Cannot erase independent tellers or references. A denied erasure records the retention basis and may be appealed to the operator. |
| protected subject | Verified identity plus subject-resolution environment | Request erasure of governed personal payloads and dependants within the resolved subject scope | Ambiguous or overlapping identity fails closed to operator review. Independent legal or safety retention requires a scoped, expiring hold. |
| operator | Authenticated local operator authority | Retract or erase local governed records within deployment policy | Cannot pretend an irreversible external effect was undone. Exceptional retention and overrides are recorded and reviewable. |
| retention authority | Signed authority named by a reference or policy | Place, renew, or release a scoped retention hold; it cannot widen disclosure | A hold has purpose, scope, expiry, and reviewer. Conflict with an erasure request preserves only the minimum encrypted payload until review. |
| policy reviewer | Authenticated governed role distinct from the initiating automation | Approve or reject ambiguous requests, holds, and appeals | The decision cites policy version, evidence, scope, expiry, and replacement or appeal path. |

The recorded decision contains requester, authentication evidence, resolved scope, permitted operation, policy version, conflicts, holds, reviewer, source head, and outcome. Closure traversal accepts only an `allowed` decision. `denied` and `pending_review` decisions cause no destructive action.

### Erasure concurrency and linearization

An allowed decision does not immediately destroy keys. It opens a stable scope-specific erasure epoch and folds the scope to `erasing`. The epoch is the linearization barrier for consumption and publication.

1. Append `erasure_started` with the authorization decision ID, canonical scope digest, epoch ID, and start head. From this append onward, audience resolution, blob retrieval, model/tool rendering, proposal publication, retries, jobs, exports, and new retention wrappers whose typed lineage intersects the scope fail closed with `erasure_in_progress`. Candidate discovery may run only when it cannot read scoped payload or publish effects.
2. Every Activity, read, proposal, Derivation, index write, job, and wrapper creation records the erasure-epoch vector observed when it began and performs compare-at-commit. Work begun before `erasure_started` may finish computation, but publication or delivery aborts when its scope intersects the new epoch. Its governed partial payload enters the epoch closure and cannot become an independently surviving source.
3. Wait until every pre-epoch in-flight consumer or writer has either committed before the start head or appended an abort/failure record. Append `erasure_closure_watermark` naming that quiescent log head. A stuck operation is cancelled or moves the erasure to operator-visible `blocked`; key destruction cannot proceed while its outcome is unknown.
4. Traverse dependencies over the immutable log through the closure watermark. If traversal discovers another scoped payload, wrapper, job, or dependant, add it and repeat until one complete pass adds nothing. Append the canonical closure digest, member IDs, and fixed-point head. A record appended after the watermark cannot depend on scoped input because compare-at-commit rejects it.
5. In one event-log transaction, append terminal object/reference tombstones, the erasure watermark, projection and snapshot invalidations, cancelled job/Trigger records, and every key-wrapper destruction instruction. This append is the irreversible semantic commit. A crash before it leaves keys readable but the scope blocked in `erasing`; recovery resumes traversal. A crash after it can only resume key destruction and rebuild; restoration is forbidden.
6. Destroy keys and managed copies, record receipts and bounded external failures, rebuild indexes/projections above the watermark, and append `erasure_completed`. Reads, jobs, restore, and retries remain blocked for the scope until completion. The same tombstone ledger blocks serving on every replica and during restore.

Race fixtures start the same read and Activity immediately before, at, and after `erasure_started`. The pre-barrier work must compare-fail or appear inside the fixed-point closure; at-barrier and post-barrier work must deny before payload rendering. Another fixture crashes immediately before and after the terminal transaction. Replay must produce, respectively, resumable `erasing` with readable encrypted keys but no serving, and irreversible tombstones with key-destruction work pending. No schedule may publish or deliver a scoped derivative after the closure watermark.

### Genesis erasure storage baseline

Stage -1 freezes the following baseline. Stage 2 implements it without selecting a weaker alternative:

- Each governed payload record has a distinct random data-encryption key. The immutable event envelope stores only ciphertext, non-sensitive routing metadata, the key-wrapper ID, and a payload commitment. Erasing one payload destroys every live wrapper for that payload key without requiring deletion of the hash-chained envelope.
- Shared Artefact bytes have one byte key and one independent authorization wrapper per live ArtefactReference or explicit retention obligation. Erasing a reference destroys its wrapper and derived-only keys. The byte key survives while another independent wrapper remains. The final wrapper's destruction makes the bytes unreadable and schedules physical deletion from primary storage, caches, and managed replicas.
- Model requests, model responses, proposal payloads, critic diagnostics, Perceptions, derived Artefacts, embeddings, descriptions, episodes, job arguments, and action arguments each use their own payload key. An implementation may pack payloads physically only when its key index can destroy and prove destruction at the same per-record granularity.
- A scoped retention hold wraps only the minimum affected payload keys under a hold-specific key. The hold records purpose, authority, scope, and expiry. It cannot provide read access or widen disclosure. Expiry or release destroys the hold wrapper before closure resumes. A pending request may be cancelled before wrapper destruction; completed destruction has no restoration path.
- The live tombstone ledger is outside restorable payload snapshots and is replicated independently. Restore first verifies the event-envelope chain, then loads the latest tombstone and key-destruction ledger, rejects snapshots below its erasure watermark, restores encrypted payloads, filters destroyed key-wrapper IDs, rebuilds projections and indexes from readable payloads, and only then opens reads or jobs. No restored copy may serve during this sequence.
- Primary encrypted backups use bounded generations. A backup generation expires no later than 30 days after replacement. An allowed erasure destroys the scoped backup key at the next daily erasure sweep and records a receipt; the 30-day bound covers deletion of now-unreadable backup media. A legal hold suspends only the named scoped key until its recorded expiry. Restore still applies the live tombstone ledger before access.
- Eval packages, test captures, and managed exports containing governed payload use separately registered owners, payload keys, and retention periods. The default retention is seven days for eval/test captures and 30 days for managed exports unless a shorter deployment policy applies. Erasure destroys their scoped keys at the same daily sweep and records deletion or recipient-notification status. A package may never seed restore.
- An export outside managed key control cannot be promised erased. The authorization decision warns before export, records recipient and scope, and an erasure request records notification and unresolved external-copy status. The system reports this as a bounded failure rather than claiming deletion.

The 24-hour sweep, seven-day package default, and 30-day backup/export bounds are initial policies over the permanent per-payload key and tombstone representation. Deployments may shorten them. Lengthening them or changing key granularity after genesis requires a new policy version and may not make previously erasable payloads less erasable.

### Adversarial erasure case matrix

This matrix is a complete cross-product over the surfaces above. Each cell names the deterministic outcome. `Deny` means that no payload reaches an uncleared context. `Inherit` means that the output retains the intersected restriction and cannot serve more broadly. `Invalidate/recompute` means that the old result cannot serve and a replacement may use only surviving authorised inputs. `Retain independent` means that only a separately authorised reference, wrapper, or copy survives. `Block` means that the operation remains incomplete and is reported rather than claimed successful. No cell is implicitly not applicable.

| Surface | Direct hidden input | Hidden influence | Missing bytes or destroyed key | Duplicate independently retained blob |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| source utterances and typed content parts | Deny rendering and source query before support aggregation. | Inherit the source restriction on every consumer. | Return the terminal tombstone; never reconstruct text or parts. | Retain independent only when another Occasion owns separately authorised content; shared byte identity does not restore this source payload. |
| source blobs and encryption keys | Deny retrieval without the reference wrapper and audience decision. | Inherit the intersection on each derived key and output. | Return `unavailable` or `erased`; no model or tool Activity begins. | Retain independent through surviving wrappers; destroy the final byte key only after the last authority ends. |
| ArtefactReferences | Deny metadata and bytes outside the reference principle. | Inherit this reference's principle through consumed-reference edges. | Fold terminally erased when its payload key is destroyed; deny restoration. | Retain independent references, captions, principles, and wrappers unchanged. |
| model-call requests and responses | Deny call construction when a direct input is uncleared. | Inherit restrictions on request, response, retry, and every output. | Block replay or retry from absent payload; preserve only the outcome tombstone. | Retain independent calls only when all their consumed references and wrappers survive. |
| accepted proposal payloads | Deny publication and ordinary proposal reads. | Inherit or invalidate/recompute the published output. | Invalidate publication payload and dependants; source-only may remain. | Retain independent proposals only when their complete lineage uses the surviving reference. |
| amended proposal payloads | Deny review and publication of the hidden draft. | Inherit across original, amendment, critic, and retry. | Invalidate/recompute from surviving authorised source; never recover an erased draft. | Retain an independent amendment only when its lineage and payload key are separate and authorised. |
| rejected and dropped proposal payloads | Deny every ordinary query and retry-context render. | Inherit into any retry or later teaching effect; otherwise erase zero-residue influence. | Return terminal tombstone; later retries start from surviving inputs only. | Retain no copy merely because identical bytes survive elsewhere; only separately authorised lineage can be retried. |
| critic diagnostics and errors | Deny source fragments and detailed diagnostics to uncleared callers. | Inherit into retries, review, queueing, and accepted output. | Invalidate affected retry context and outputs; retain only the error class tombstone. | Retain a diagnostic only when it consumed the independently authorised reference and has its own live key. |
| Activity input and access records | Deny the Activity before content rendering; retain an operator-only denial envelope. | Inherit restrictions and purpose on the Activity and outputs. | Block execution or replay; an absent payload never becomes an allow. | Retain independent access only through the surviving reference's decision and wrapper. |
| derived Artefacts and transform payloads | Deny transform input and create no output. | Inherit the intersection on output wrapper, reference, and Derivation. | Invalidate selectors and dependants; shred derived-only output keys. | Retain independent output only when a separate Derivation and authority do not depend on the erased reference. |
| Perceptions, OCR, captions, and regions | Deny creation or read outside the consumed reference's audience. | Inherit into output, indexes, Assertions, and reinspection. | Invalidate/recompute; never regenerate during replay from missing bytes. | Retain independent Perception only when it consumed the surviving reference under its own access decision. |
| embeddings, vector rows, lexical indexes, and caches | Deny insertion into an audience-broader index or result. | Inherit partition/restriction metadata; hidden vectors cannot affect visible rank. | Delete and rebuild before serving. | Retain independent rows only when keyed to surviving lineage; byte equality alone is insufficient. |
| Assertions and Attestations or SourceAuthorities | Deny the hidden support/source before read or action. | Inherit into any derived conclusion, or recompute from visible support only. | Fold erased/unsupported and invalidate dependants without changing stable IDs. | Retain independent support only when its Attestation or SourceAuthority cites surviving authorised lineage. |
| Derivations and support/ranking projections | Deny output whose direct input is uncleared. | Inherit the full intersection; no visible ordinal changes from hidden support. | Invalidate/recompute from surviving inputs and a new Derivation ID. | Retain a conclusion only through a newly recorded independent Derivation over surviving lineage. |
| descriptions, episodes, and working notes | Deny generation or rendering from direct hidden input. | Inherit for gated syntheses; descriptions require public inputs and otherwise invalidate. | Delete or invalidate/recompute; never use an erased note as retry context. | Retain independent prose only when all its recorded input edges survive independently. |
| queue items, leases, retries, and poison records | Deny enqueueing into a broader authority domain. | Inherit restriction on arguments, diagnostics, worker, and output. | Cancel and tombstone; compare-at-commit rejects missing arguments. | Retain an independent job only when its key and complete input lineage name the surviving reference. |
| rendered-read and access-accounting records | Deny payload rendering while recording only a permitted denial trace. | Inherit restriction on detailed trace payload; counts cannot leak hidden cardinality. | Retain the access class and tombstone, not erased rendered content. | Retain separate access records for the surviving reference without re-authorising the erased one. |
| Triggers and scheduled Tasks | Deny arming or firing from uncleared direct input. | Inherit into condition, arguments, and initiated action; otherwise cancel. | Cancel and remove scheduler state; restore never re-arms. | Retain independent Task/Trigger only when its full arguments and source lineage use the surviving reference. |
| initiated actions and external-effect records | Deny initiation before an uncleared argument leaves the system. | Inherit into pending payload and destination authorization; cancel when it cannot. | Cancel pending work; record completed external effects and remediation as an explicit blocker. | Retain independent completed effects as history, but no surviving blob authority authorises a new effect. |
| console and operator traces | Deny ordinary console rendering; operator audit uses the restricted capability. | Inherit trace retention and viewer restrictions. | Purge managed payload caches; retain permitted tombstone and deletion receipt. | Retain an independent trace only for the surviving reference and its own audience decision. |
| snapshots and materialised projections | Deny serving a snapshot containing uncleared direct input. | Inherit partitioning or invalidate the snapshot when influence cannot be separated. | Reject snapshots below the erasure watermark and rebuild before reads. | Retain/rebuild only independently authorised rows; shared physical bytes do not preserve erased projections. |
| eval packages and test captures | Deny capture without separate explicit authorization. | Inherit package encryption, owner, and retention; never feed a broader run. | Destroy package key and record receipt; packages never seed restore. | Retain a separately authorised package only when its lineage uses the surviving reference and its retention remains live. |
| exports and API downloads | Deny export before delivery when direct input is uncleared. | Inherit managed-link restrictions and recipient scope; otherwise block. | Revoke managed copies; notify and record unresolved status for uncontrolled copies. | Retain an independently authorised export as an external copy with its own recipient and status; it grants no local reference authority. |
| backups | Deny restoration into a serving system before authorization and tombstone filtering. | Inherit encryption scope and hold; hidden payload never affects rebuilt visible projections. | Destroy scoped keys or expire media; block serving until ledger application and rebuild finish. | Retain independently wrapped bytes in backup only while a surviving reference or hold remains; erase the scoped wrapper, not the independent one. |

An Artefact's minted ID is stable internal identity. Verified digest assertions locate and deduplicate bytes; neither the ID nor a digest grants authorisation. The current system's blob store is persistent and must be backed up with the log ([current storage contract](../docs/events-and-storage.md#blob-store)); any route that treats a hash as a bearer capability is a current-system limitation. The successor requires authenticated retrieval or short-lived signed access. A shared verified byte identity does not collapse sharing authority: identical bytes supplied on two Occasions produce two ArtefactReferences. Erasing one reference removes its support and authorisation only. Physical bytes are collected or shredded only after no surviving reference or retention obligation authorises them.

Derived outputs lose erased support and are re-evaluated against what remains. A conclusion that can survive from independent, authorised inputs may remain only as a newly recorded Derivation with replacement lineage; one that encodes only erased input must not. The baseline key, payload, closure, reference-withdrawal, invalidation, snapshot, restore, export, package, and backup behavior is required before Stage 2 can activate any restricted source or Artefact. Later privacy stages may add policies and operational tooling, but they cannot introduce basic forgetting after successor genesis.

## Inter-agent status and revocation

An incoming inter-agent claim is a quoted Assertion with an Attestation naming the sending agent and preserving the travelling transmission principle and provenance chain. A remote content address is identity evidence, not an executable revocation channel.

Retraction uses an authenticated local subscription/status protocol. The local store records the remote issuer, stable remote Attestation identity, verification material, a monotonically increasing issuer notice sequence, the last verified status, and each signed revocation notice. Each subscription records a freshness lease, polling deadline, allowed clock skew, and last successful check. A notice with an old or duplicate sequence is idempotently ignored; a gap quarantines status pending fetch.

When the freshness lease expires, the fold moves from `current` to `stale` at the recorded deadline. Stale remote support is removed from every actionable support, ranking, Derivation, and initiated-action projection by default. Dependent Derivations become invalid or quarantined until recomputed without it. Ordinary conversational reads may show only an explicitly stale, non-actionable quoted record when its transmission principle still clears; they cannot present it as current support. A valid revocation appends a local Attestation-retraction transition. Key rotation is accepted only through a signed continuity or operator-authorised replacement record, and replay applies the same notice sequence and lease deadlines. Polling recovery can restore `current` only with a newer signed status. The remote system cannot rewrite local history, and a bare content hash never causes propagation by itself.
