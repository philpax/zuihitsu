# Confidence register

Normative chapters specify the proposed successor architecture. Their claims do not have equal support. This register records the evidence status, unresolved decisions, and gates for each load-bearing claim.

## Status vocabulary

The following table is the canonical mapping across evidence, numbered-stage, gate, and lifecycle vocabulary. A row may combine one evidence status with one blocking or lifecycle status. Coverage status describes only whether a known failure is representable. It never upgrades evidence. Numbered stages are never selected, skipped, or assigned a status in the genesis selection record. Conditional selection applies to capability entries, whose named gate is the capability ID.

| Requested distinction | Canonical status | Permitted aliases and combinations | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| observed evidence | `Observed` | May combine with `Corroborated`, `Genesis-blocking`, or `Stage-gating` | Established by direct measurement, code, tests, or current-system documentation. |
| verified evidence | `Verified` | `Verified, corroborated` when independent sources agree | Checked against a fetched primary source by the adversarial verification pass. |
| corroborated evidence | `Corroborated` | May qualify `Observed` or `Verified` | Multiple independent sources agree. |
| single-source evidence | `Single-source` | May combine with `Open experiment` | Rests on one study or system and is unreplicated. |
| decided policy | `Decided` | May combine with any lifecycle class | An operator or architecture decision. Evidence can support the premise, never prove the decision. |
| design synthesis | `Synthesis` | May combine with `Genesis-blocking`, `Stage-gating`, or `Safely deferred` | An inference by this design that the cited sources do not themselves make. |
| unvalidated hypothesis | `Unvalidated hypothesis` | `Open` in older chapter tables | A specified claim with named alternatives that lacks decisive evidence. It has one unresolved-register row unless explicitly settled or declined. |
| structural solution | `Closed structurally` | Coverage-only; never an evidence status | The successor representation makes the named historical failure unrepresentable. |
| design answer without validation | `Answered in design, not yet validated` | Coverage-only | A mechanism is specified but its behaviour or economics has not passed its gate. |
| genesis-freeze blocker | `Genesis-blocking` | Lifecycle class | The genesis freeze cannot approve the successor without this record shape, rule, required input, and executable oracle. The row names the exact canonical owner, stage or capability, evidence, and oracle IDs. |
| evidence-stage blocker | `Evidence-stage blocking` | May qualify a measurement or experimental increment | A dependent pre-genesis experiment cannot produce valid evidence until the named prerequisite or preregistered decision exists. This refers to a numbered stage or evidence package, never a capability selection. |
| gated capability | `Stage-gating` | `Gated extension` in README and overview | A capability remains disabled until the named activation gate for its `capability:<name>` passes. It does not block genesis unless the selection record marks it `initial_policy`. |
| safely deferred capability | `Safely deferred` | May later become `Stage-gating` | Genesis records every required input, disabled behaviour, and additive seam, so later addition does not reinterpret history. |
| open experiment | `Open experiment` | A safely deferred capability with no activation commitment | Not part of the committed architecture until its independent experiment and named activation gate pass. |
| required substrate | `required_substrate` | Selection-record status for capabilities only | A capability seam is permanent and required even when no worker or policy is active. Numbered stages use no selection status. |
| initial policy | `initial_policy` | Selection-record status for capabilities only | The operator selects the capability's policy and version for the first real genesis. Its evidence and prerequisite closure block the freeze. |
| activation gate | `activation_gate` | Selection-record status for capabilities only | The capability's inputs and disabled seam are recorded, but active behaviour waits for its independent named gate. |
| settled scope exclusion | `Declined` | Selection-record status `declined`; not unresolved | Deliberately excluded. The row names the future condition that would reopen it. |

## The verification pass

Two adversarial passes ran over the research report before the design was taken up ([`research/2026-07-24/verification/`](research/2026-07-24/verification/)). Of its load-bearing cited claims: 29 confirmed against fetched primary sources, 5 corrected, 0 unsupported, 1 unreachable. Every future-dated citation was fetched directly rather than judged from memory, and all resolved to real sources supporting the claims as reported.

The five corrections are folded into the chapters. They were: the identification of provenance polynomials with justification labels relabelled as this design's own synthesis rather than a claim the source makes; the conservatism about subjective logic extended from its fusion operators to its evidence-count mapping; the severance description tightened so re-derivation is a record-time pass rather than part of the fold; the drift figures for the long-running accumulator made precise, being a monotonic decline in newly promoted claims rather than a cliff; and the causal story for forced-choice elicitation tempered to distinguish omission variance from content variance.

## Unresolved-item register

`Evidence-stage blocking` means that a dependent pre-genesis experiment cannot proceed without its prerequisite or preregistered rule. `Genesis-blocking` means that the genesis freeze cannot approve the successor without the record shape, rule, required input, and executable oracle named in the row. `Stage-gating` means that a capability remains disabled until the named gate for its canonical capability ID passes. `Safely deferred` means that the first real genesis records every input, disabled-behaviour oracle, and additive seam needed to add the capability without reinterpreting history. Numbered stages are always run; they have no selection status.

Every row has one primary canonical owner. `Primary stage or capability` is exactly one `stage:<n>` or `capability:<name>` ID. Secondary prerequisites are references only and do not create a second owner. A capability row names its gate explicitly when the roadmap assigns one. Genesis-blocking rows name exact `owner:`, stage or capability, `evidence:`, and `oracle:` IDs. The oracle register in [`evolution.md`](evolution.md) must resolve each oracle to one executable test or measurement.

## Ledger, seed, freshness, and initial-policy decisions

These four decisions are part of the candidate contract and are not inferred from an implementation that happens to exist before genesis. Each unresolved decision keeps a stable ID, an exact owner, retained evidence, and an executable oracle. The freeze cannot replace any of these records with a prose assertion.

### Candidate authoritative-ledger architecture and recovery authority

`unresolved:authoritative-ledger-architecture` owns the comparison of candidate ledger designs. The ledger is the serving deployment's current authoritative position for terminal erasure and restore filtering. It is an append-only, monotonically ordered record kept beside the serving log and included in every backup. A bundled backup ledger is restore material, not authority. The candidate comparison covers replication, loss, rollback, disaster recovery, authority continuity, fail-closed serving, and the consequences of an unverifiable current position.

`unresolved:recovery-authority-continuity` owns the recovery rule. Restore acquires the single-writer lock before reading the current authoritative ledger or touching managed live state. While holding it, restore obtains a verifiable position, filters and rebuilds with serving blocked, opens serving atomically, and releases the lock only after the serving transition. Missing or unverifiable current authority applies no position and keeps serving blocked. A restored snapshot or backup cannot establish authority by itself. The corresponding ledger and erasure obligations are `unresolved:erasure-authority-storage-restore` and `obligation:erasure-non-restoration`.

The architecture remains a candidate until `stage:0` compares the alternatives and `stage:6` exercises the selected contract. The permanence requirement is not candidate-dependent: erased data cannot regain authority, an older backup cannot undo a newer tombstone, and a failed authority check cannot open serving.

### Successor seed definitions

`unresolved:successor-seed-definitions` owns the exact genesis definition manifest. The seed relation set is the minimum required by permanent mechanics: identity, participation, composition, membership, placement, origin, operatorship, and acquaintance. The manifest also includes the universal Event-role parents and the identity-bearing definition slots required by the object model, including modality and transmission-principle definitions whose richer policies may remain disabled. If a presentation or other definition is required by a permanent projection, the manifest records it explicitly rather than treating it as an implicit seed.

The seed is minimal and governed. It does not preload social or environmental knowledge. Every seed has a stable ID, version, constraints, activation authority, and replay semantics. A missing or ambiguous seed definition leaves the dependent write source-only or inactive; it never causes the agent to coin an unregistered relation. `stage:4` records the compared set, and `oracle:seed-definition-set` proves that the selected manifest is complete, versioned, and replayable.

### Inter-agent freshness versus remote activation

`unresolved:inter-agent-freshness-record` is a required substrate seam and is independent of whether remote content is initially actionable. An incoming inter-agent claim is a quoted Assertion. Its local record preserves the remote issuer, stable remote Attestation identity, verification material, monotonic issuer notice sequence, signed status, freshness lease, polling deadline, key continuity, last verified status, stale transition, and source head. Old or duplicate notices are idempotent. Sequence gaps quarantine status. Lease expiry folds to `stale`, and stale remote support is removed from actionable support, ranking, Derivation, and initiated-action projections by default. A remote content address never acts as a revocation channel.

`unresolved:remote-actionable-activation` is a separate capability. Its `gate:remote-actionable-status` may admit remote support only after the freshness seam exists and its independent audience, subject-guard, signature, key-continuity, revocation, and stale-state evidence passes. Freshness records can therefore be retained at genesis while remote support remains disabled. A later activation adds an actionable projection; it does not reinterpret a historical quoted Assertion or turn a stale record into current support.

### Initial-policy matrix and selection record

`unresolved:initial-policy-matrix` owns the operator's capability selection record. Numbered stages `stage:0` through `stage:12` are always run and have no status, selection, or skip field. The selection record contains capability entries only:

```text
capability_id: capability:<name>
status: required_substrate | initial_policy | activation_gate | declined
policy_version: <required for initial_policy, otherwise null>
prerequisite_capability_ids: [capability:<name>, ...]
evidence_ids: [evidence:<name>, ...]
executable_oracle_ids: [oracle:<name>, ...]
disabled_behaviour_oracle_id: oracle:<name>
additive_seam_reference: <stage or capability contract>
operator_decision: <recorded decision>
decision_source_head: <log position>
```

The matrix is capability-level, not stage-level. Required substrate entries remain permanent even when their worker or policy is disabled. An `initial_policy` entry and its transitive prerequisite closure block the freeze and are exercised by the rehearsal. An `activation_gate` or `declined` entry does not block genesis, but it must have a passing disabled-behaviour oracle and an additive seam. A later operator selection change recomputes the closure and invalidates dependent evidence.

The documentation change does not select the operator's initial capabilities. Before dependent initial-policy evidence runs, the operator records one versioned matrix from the following capability families and their canonical gates:

## Claims deliberately not made

- That the design closes the eleven surveyed failures. It closes six structurally and answers five in design without validation. See [`coverage.md`](coverage.md).
- That any benchmark number here is a target. The one composite memory benchmark surveyed was found by independent audit to have a materially wrong answer key and a judge that accepted most intentionally wrong answers. Component success is measured by structural oracles against this system's own log, not by a self-reported score.
- That autonomy means no human attention. It means exception-triggered attention with a queue that shrinks per fact as the store grows.
- That the neural writer is verified for truth. The critics check well-formedness, typing, consistency, and whether an extracted value is traceable to a source locator. That check establishes groundedness rather than truth. A well-typed falsehood can still pass at write time. Runtime faithfulness checking remains unsolved.
